Travel Notes

Selected insights

I collect the small design and business decisions I notice while travelling. These are the ones I keep thinking about.

New York
USA · 40.7°N 74.0°W
  • A bigger market isn't just a bigger WarsawLaunching Swayze in New York meant dealing with a much larger area, more fragmented audiences and stronger competition. Scaling required a different marketing playbook, not just more budget.
  • Local traction doesn't equal global validationA product can work well in one city and still struggle elsewhere. New York forced us to separate what was truly scalable from what worked because we understood our home market particularly well.
  • Competition forces clarityIn a crowded market, being new is not enough. Strong competition made us think much harder about what was genuinely different about the product and why someone should choose us.
London
UK · 51.5°N 0.1°W
  • Trademark before tractionWhile building Swayze, we received a cease-and-desist letter related to our brand name. It taught me to treat trademark research as an early business decision, not something to deal with later.
  • A brand name becomes an asset quicklyNaming feels creative at first, but once reputation, customers and money become attached to it, the name becomes something worth protecting.
  • Old interfaces can outlive their original functionLondon's red phone booths are still instantly recognisable even though their original purpose has largely disappeared. Some have been repurposed, which made me think about how strong symbols can survive even when the function changes.
Dubai
UAE · 25.2°N 55.3°E
  • Speed is designed upstreamFive-minute grocery delivery looks like a delivery feature, but the real speed comes from inventory and logistics being positioned close to demand before an order is placed.
  • The environment shapes innovationDubai made me notice how much regulation, infrastructure and access to resources influence what companies are able to build. Innovation speed is not only about founders or technology.
  • Strong systems support themselvesI learned about groups of plants designed to help each other survive in difficult environments. It reminded me that resilient systems often work because individual components make the others stronger.
Tokyo
Japan · 35.7°N 139.7°E
  • The best design answers the question before you ask itInstructions and contextual guidance often appeared exactly where confusion could happen. Good design reduced the need to stop and search for help.
  • Warnings don't have to threatenPublic instructions often used friendly characters and softer communication instead of aggressive warning language. Compliance does not always require anxiety.
  • Navigation can exist beyond the screenDifferent sounds at pedestrian crossings communicate direction without requiring visual attention. Accessibility becomes part of the environment itself.
